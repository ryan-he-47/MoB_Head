# Numerical Stability and Mode-Collapse Control for Gaussian-Mixture MDN Heads — A Cited Survey

> 归档说明：本文献调研由 librarian 子代理于 2026-08-31 完成，作为 `docs/03-experiment-plan.md` 数值稳定性设计的依据。所有论断均附文献或代码出处；标注 **[extrapolation]** 的为代理在已证实事实上的推理外推。

**Scope**: MDN (Mixture Density Network) heads trained by average NLL, spherical Gaussians, 32 components, 17-dim targets. All claims carry a paper (title + year) or a concrete code/doc URL. Items marked **[extrapolation]** are my own reasoning on top of cited facts, not established results.

## 1. Root causes of numerical instability in GMM/MDN NLL training

- **σ→0 singularity (unbounded likelihood / degeneracy)**. The classical result: for a normal mixture, if one component mean coincides with a data point and its variance → 0, the log-likelihood grows without bound, so a global MLE does not exist.
  - Kiefer & Wolfowitz, *Consistency of the Maximum Likelihood Estimator in the Presence of Infinitely Many Incidental Parameters*, Ann. Math. Statist. 27(4):887–906 (1956) — first documented the failure for 2-Gaussian mixtures ([ProjectEuclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-27/issue-4/Consistency-of-the-Maximum-Likelihood-Estimator-in-the-Presence-of/10.1214/aoms/1177728066.full)).
  - Redner & Walker, *Mixture Densities, Maximum Likelihood and the EM Algorithm*, SIAM Review 26(2):195–239 (1984) — standard survey; states the likelihood is unbounded when "one of the mixture means coincides with a sample observation and the corresponding variance tends to zero", attributes it to Kiefer–Wolfowitz, and reviews remedies (parameter constraints, penalty terms) ([PDF](https://users.wpi.edu/~walker/Papers/mixtures-mle-em%2CSIREV_26%2C1984%2C195-239.pdf)).
  - Biernacki & Chrétien, *Degeneracy in the maximum likelihood estimation of univariate Gaussian mixtures with EM*, Statistics & Probability Letters 61(4):373–382 (2003) — proves there is a **domain of attraction around degeneracy** and EM converges there **at least exponentially fast** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0167-7152(02)00396-6)). In an MDN trained by SGD the same degeneracy appears as σ shrinking until the NLL explodes.
  - Bishop, *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning* (2006), §9.2.1 "Singularities" — the canonical ML-text treatment for GMMs.
- **MDN-specific variant**: Bishop's 1994 MDN paper itself uses σ = exp(z) explicitly because this "has the additional benefit of avoiding pathological configurations in which one or more of the variances goes to zero" ([Bishop 1994, NCRG/94-004, Eq. 26](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/bishop-ncrg-94-004.pdf)). The exp parameterization removes σ ≤ 0 but not the σ→0 pathology, and exp can overflow.
- **log-sum-exp overflow in the mixture sum**: the NLL requires log Σₖ πₖ·N(x|μₖ,σₖ); naive exp of large positive log-densities (tiny σ) overflows, naive exp of large negative values underflows to 0 and log(0) → −∞/NaN.
  - Canonical treatment: the "log-sum-exp trick" — Murphy, *Machine Learning: A Probabilistic Perspective* (2012), §3.5.2; the fused softmax+logloss discussion in Goodfellow et al., *Deep Learning* (2016), §4.1/§6.2.
  - Documented in practice: Letzelter et al., *Winner-takes-all learners are geometry-aware conditional density estimators* (arXiv:2406.04706, 2024) report "we faced stability issues when training MDN (numerical overflows in log-likelihood computation)" ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04706v1)).
- **Division by near-zero σ ⇒ huge, oscillating gradients**: LayoutMCL (Nguyen et al., *Diverse Multimedia Layout Generation with Multi Choice Learning*, arXiv:2301.06629, 2023) names the mechanism explicitly: "we still experience numerical instabilities for layouts caused by divisions of very small variances resulting in large gradients" ([PDF](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06629v1.pdf)).
- **Half-precision arithmetic**: in fp16, exp(z) overflows above ~65504 (z ≈ 11.1); exp underflows below ~6e-5 (z ≈ −9.7), so any mixture term below that underflows to 0 and the logsumexp silently drops all but the max term; `(x−μ)²/(2σ²)` overflows for σ ≲ 1e-2. Micikevicius et al., *Mixed Precision Training*, ICLR (2018) — the canonical treatment (loss scaling, fp32 master weights, fp32 accumulation) ([arXiv:1710.03740](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03740)). PyTorch's autocast policy is precisely designed around this: under autocast, **loss functions, log, exp, pow and softmax run in fp32** even when the network runs fp16 ([PyTorch amp docs](https://pytorch.org/docs/stable/amp.html); op policy lists in [`docs/source/amp.md`](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/main/docs/source/amp.md)); ops not in any policy list run in the input dtype with fp16 accumulation — so a hand-written fp16 NLL gets *none* of that protection.
- **Dimensionality**: Makansi et al., *Overcoming Limitations of Mixture Density Networks* (CVPR 2019): "Optimizing all parameters jointly in MDNs is difficult, **becomes numerically unstable in higher dimensions**, and suffers from degenerate predictions" ([arXiv:1906.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03631)). This is their motivation for abandoning direct MDN NLL training in favor of sampling-and-fitting.
- **Gradient-pathology of NLL (not a singularity, but instability-relevant)**: Seitzer et al., *On the Pitfalls of Heteroscedastic Uncertainty Estimation with Probabilistic Neural Networks*, ICLR (2022): NLL scales each point's gradient by the inverse predicted variance, so high-variance/ill-fitted regions are progressively *undersampled* by the optimizer — stable but poor fixed points ([arXiv:2203.09168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09168)). Cited in the MDN context by [arXiv:2406.04706](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04706v1) as "low contribution to the gradient of points with high predictive variance".
- **Practical note from canonical work**: Graves, *Generating Sequences With Recurrent Neural Networks* (2013): with exp-parameterized σ, "the networks sometimes had numerical problems late on in training", and gradient clipping "proved vital for numerical stability" ([arXiv:1308.0850](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0850), §4.1).

## 2. Standard fixes for numerical instability (with citations)

- **Variance floors (σ ≥ σ_min)** — the classical constraint remedy.
  - Hathaway (1983, *Constrained maximum-likelihood estimation for a mixture of m univariate normal distributions*, Univ. South Carolina Tech. Rep.) and Redner's penalty approach are both reviewed by Redner & Walker (1984) as the two classical cures.
  - Biernacki & Chrétien (2003): constraining σₖ² > α > 0 "the likelihood function is bounded" — and, as an alternative, random restarts ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0167-7152(02)00396-6)).
  - Production MDN code: magenta sketch-rnn guards the density with `-tf.log(result1 + 1e-6)  # avoid log(0)` ([magenta/models/sketch_rnn/model.py#L278](https://github.com/magenta/magenta/blob/main/magenta/models/sketch_rnn/model.py#L278)); the modern PyTorch re-implementation clamps `sigma_x = torch.clamp_min(sigma_x, 1e-5)` and ρ to ±(1−1e-5) "to avoid getting NaN"s ([labmlai sketch_rnn](https://github.com/labmlai/annotated_deep_learning_paper_implementations/blob/90e21b5a/labml_nn/sketch_rnn/__init__.py#L165-L167)).
  - scikit-learn's `GaussianMixture(reg_covar=...)` is the canonical EM-side "add εI to the covariance diagonal" parameter ([docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.mixture.GaussianMixture.html)).
- **Log-variance parameterization + clamping** — emit z = log σ (or log σ²), clamp the *logit* to [log σ_min, log σ_max], then σ = exp(z).
  - σ = exp(z): Bishop 1994 (quote above, [NCRG/94-004](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/bishop-ncrg-94-004.pdf)); Graves 2013, Eq. (21) ([arXiv:1308.0850](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0850)).
  - Upper-bound clamp on σ: Makansi et al. (2019), supplementary material: "To stabilize the training **we set an upper bound on σ, which we increase during training**" ([arXiv:1906.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03631)). This is also a training-schedule/annealing device.
- **Softplus / bounded-scale parameterization**: σ = softplus(z) + ε guarantees σ > ε without exp overflow. (No canonical paper; standard practice in modern probabilistic code, e.g. softplus-based scale layers. **Label**: widely used repo-level practice; the labml clamp above is the verified open-source instance of the same idea.)
- **Log-space loss arithmetic** — compute per-component log-density, add log πₖ, then `logsumexp`; never exponentiate before the outer log.
  - World Models' MDN-RNN code does exactly this: `v = logmix + tf_lognormal(y, mean, logstd); v = tf.reduce_logsumexp(v, 1); return -tf.reduce_mean(v)` ([hardmaru/WorldModelsExperiments, carracing/rnn/rnn.py#L139](https://github.com/hardmaru/WorldModelsExperiments/blob/master/carracing/rnn/rnn.py#L139)).
  - Pyro's `MixtureOfDiagNormals.log_prob` is the reference-quality PyTorch version: it subtracts the per-component max before `torch.logsumexp` and keeps everything in log space ([pyro/distributions/diag_normal_mixture.py](https://github.com/pyro-ppl/pyro/blob/dev/pyro/distributions/diag_normal_mixture.py#L95-L106)); Pyro: Bingham et al., *Pyro: Deep Universal Probabilistic Programming* (2019, arXiv:1810.09538).
  - TensorFlow Probability `MixtureSameFamily` likewise mixes via log_softmax in log space ([tfp/distributions/mixture_same_family.py](https://github.com/tensorflow/probability/blob/main/tensorflow_probability/python/distributions/mixture_same_family.py#L678)).
- **Gradient clipping on the NLL path**: Graves 2013 — output gradients clipped to [−100, 100], recurrent to [−10, 10], "vital for numerical stability" ([arXiv:1308.0850](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0850)); sketch-rnn uses `grad_clip=1.0` by default ([magenta sketch_rnn model.py](https://github.com/magenta/magenta/blob/master/magenta/models/sketch_rnn/model.py)).
- **fp32 loss computation / mixed precision hygiene**: compute the entire NLL (log densities, logsumexp, reduction) in fp32 outside autocast, even with an fp16 backbone; keep fp32 master weights and use loss scaling for fp16 gradient updates (Micikevicius et al., ICLR 2018). Under PyTorch autocast, `log`, `exp`, `pow` and loss ops are already promoted to fp32, but a hand-written loss composed of "unlisted" ops (e.g. `mean` over fp16 tensors, or `Normal.log_prob` on fp16 tensors) is **not** — PyTorch docs: "Ops not listed below do not go through autocasting. They run in the type defined by their inputs" ([docs/source/amp.md](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/main/docs/source/amp.md)). Note `torch.distributions.Normal.log_prob` computes `-(value-loc)**2/(2*var) - log_scale - const` in the input dtype ([torch/distributions/normal.py](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/main/torch/distributions/normal.py#L94-L102)) — feed it fp32 tensors.
- **Training schedule / annealing of σ**: Makansi et al. (2019) also train means first, then variances, then jointly ("sequentially learn first the means, then the variances and finally all parameters jointly"), and their MDNs *still* collapse ([arXiv:1906.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03631), §6.4) — i.e., schedules help stability but do not solve collapse (next section).
- **Temperature scaling of component logits**: at *sampling/decode* time, dividing the softmax logits by τ sharpens the component selection, and scaling σ² by τ controls per-component spread — sketch-rnn: "Π̂ₖ → Π̂ₖ/τ, σₓ² → σₓ²τ" (Ha & Eck, *A Neural Representation of Sketch Drawings*, arXiv:1704.03477, 2017, Eq. 8); Graves 2013's bias parameter b is the same device (Eqs. 61–62). At *training* time, logit temperature is the deterministic-annealing mechanism (see §4).

## 3. Root causes of mode collapse / component-weight shrinkage in MDNs

- **Winner-takes-all / mode-competition dynamics**: under NLL, the mixture is trained as a soft competition; components that lag get lower responsibility and then less gradient, so the system locks into a solution using a subset of components. Documented and named "mode collapse" for MDNs by Makansi et al. (2019): "Classical mixture density networks suffer from frequent mode collapse"; their experiments show collapse *even with careful sequential initialization* ([arXiv:1906.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03631)).
- **Weight shrinkage as the collapse mechanism**: Zhou et al., *Movement Primitive Learning and Generalization using Mixture Density Networks* (KIT, 2020, IEEE RA-L/IROS): "By maximizing the likelihood of all demonstrations, MDN tends to output a **small mixing coefficient** for the mixture component, which corresponds to the mode with less associated training data… a subset of components overfit all demonstrations… only a subset of them is used, which corresponds to the local minima of the NLL" — they distinguish *mode collapse* (a mode's weight shrinks because its demonstration set is small) from *model collapse* (components die entirely) ([PDF](https://h2t.iar.kit.edu/pdf/Zhou2020.pdf)).
- **Gradient starvation** (the general mechanism that makes dead components stay dead): Pezeshki et al., *Gradient Starvation: A Learning Proclivity in Neural Networks*, NeurIPS (2021) — once a feature/predictor wins, the loss no longer provides gradient to the losers ([arXiv:2011.09468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09468)). For mixtures this is the responsibility-vanishing loop.
- **NLL's variance-weighted gradients**: Seitzer et al. (2022): NLL down-weights exactly the poorly-fitted regions, so a component that initially covers its region poorly is progressively starved — "initially badly-fit regions receive increasingly less weight in the loss which results in premature convergence" ([arXiv:2203.09168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09168)).
- **Overestimated K / dead components from initialization**: Kreutz, Mühlhäuser & Sanchez Guinea, *Common Sense Initialization of Mixture Density Networks for Motion Planning with Overestimated Number of Components* (ICLR 2024, [OpenReview C6EHiLaiBT](https://openreview.net/forum?id=C6EHiLaiBT)): with more components than modes, random init leaves components far from any data, they never receive responsibility, and collapse is near-certain.
- **Overconfidence/overfitting**: Hjorth & Nabney, *Regularisation of Mixture Density Networks* (ICANN 1999): ML-trained MDNs overfit (motivating Bayesian regularization) and their adaptive-kernel experiments found "the variance is underestimated" ([PDF](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/1251/1/Ninth_International_Conference_Artificial_Neural_Networks_ICANN_99.pdf)). Makansi et al. (2019): "MDNs are usually prone to overfitting, which requires special regularization techniques and results in mode collapse".
- **"One component smears across multiple modes" (under-splitting)** — the complementary failure: a single component with σ large enough to cover several nearby modes wins by averaging them; it is especially attractive when the modes are close or rare. Documented in the multi-hypothesis literature as the "averaging problem" (hypotheses landing "in an unacceptable central region between ground truths") — Nguyen et al. 2023 ([arXiv:2301.06629](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06629v1.pdf)); and the WTA-theory literature connects WTA/MDN training to (centroidal) Voronoi tessellation quantization, which is exactly the "each hypothesis owns a cell" picture where undersized K smears cells — Letzelter et al. 2024 ([arXiv:2406.04706](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04706v1)).

## 4. Standard fixes for collapse / weight shrinkage (with citations)

- **Entropy regularization on mixture weights** — add a cost that flattens π (equivalently: maximize H(π), or KL(π ‖ uniform)):
  - Zhou et al. (2020) is the direct MDN precedent: they add a **negative model entropy cost** (over the component-conditional responsibilities, uniform prior p(qᵢ) ∝ M⁻¹) with weights w_NLL = 1, w_model = 50 and show it prevents both mode and model collapse in MDN ([PDF](https://h2t.iar.kit.edu/pdf/Zhou2020.pdf)).
  - The MoE analogue, proven at scale: auxiliary **load-balancing loss** = mean-squared coefficient of variation of per-example routing probabilities, pushing weights toward uniform — Shazeer et al., *Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts Layer* (2017, arXiv:1701.06538); Fedus et al., *Switch Transformers* (2021, arXiv:2101.03961).
- **KL/annealing schemes**:
  - **Deterministic annealing EM (DAEM)** — a temperature β on the posterior responsibilities (equivalently: mixture logits scaled by β, i.e. training-time temperature on component logits), starting at high temperature where the objective is unimodal and annealing to β=1. Ueda & Nakano, *Deterministic annealing EM algorithm*, Neural Networks 11(2) (1998), derived "by using the principle of maximum entropy" ([DOI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0893608097001330)); Ueda & Nakano, *Deterministic Annealing Variant of the EM Algorithm*, NeurIPS (1994) ([PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/1994/file/92262bf907af914b95a0fc33c3f33bf6-Paper.pdf)); Rose, *Deterministic Annealing for Clustering, Compression, Classification, Regression, and Related Optimization Problems*, Proc. IEEE 86(11) (1998).
  - **Annealed soft-assignment for neural multi-hypothesis heads**: aWTA — anneal a softmin/Boltzmann assignment temperature from high to zero during training, "the number of effective modes increases" (Saadat et al., *Annealed Winner-Takes-All for Motion Forecasting*, 2024, [arXiv:2409.11172](https://hal.science/hal-05079079v1/file/2409.11172v3.pdf)).
  - Makansi et al. (2019) use a schedule (means → variances → joint, plus an annealed σ upper bound) — schedule-as-annealing ([arXiv:1906.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03631)).
- **Initialization heuristics**:
  - k-means / k-means++ init of means: canonical for GMM fitting — scikit-learn `GaussianMixture(init_params='kmeans')` default ([docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.mixture.GaussianMixture.html)); textbook treatment in Bishop PRML (2006), §9.2.2.
  - MDN-specific kernel init guidelines: Hjorth & Nabney (1999) — place kernel means uniformly over the target range; pick variance "as small as possible" while still letting the mixture cover the range; they give an **analytic optimal variance for two adjacent kernels as a function of their spacing d** (σ* ≈ 0.56·d; formula reconstructed from the OCR of the report, [PDF](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/4334/1/NCRG_99_004.pdf), §2.2) — directly relevant to the σ-vs-vertex-spacing question.
  - Pretraining for overestimated K: **Common Sense Initialization** (CSI) — pretrain the MDN with a WTA-style objective so each component is anchored to data before NLL training (Kreutz et al., ICLR 2024).
  - Greedy component growth instead of fixed K: Vlassis & Likas, *A Greedy EM Algorithm for Gaussian Mixture Learning*, Neural Processing Letters 15(1):77–87 (2002) — start with 1 component, split/add until K, avoiding bad local optima and dead components ([DOI record](https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/11066)).
- **Covariance regularization**: εI ridge on covariance diagonals (`reg_covar`) — scikit-learn ([docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.mixture.GaussianMixture.html)); equivalent in MDN to the σ floor of §2.
- **Minimum-probability clamps on weights** — direct clamp πₖ ≥ π_min (then renormalize) is a **repo-level heuristic**; no canonical paper. The principled replacements are: (a) entropy cost (Zhou et al. 2020), (b) Bayesian priors — Svensén & Bishop, *Robust Bayesian mixture modelling*, Neurocomputing 64:235–252 (2005): with variational inference "singularities of the kind associated with maximum likelihood are absent, and **surplus components revert to the prior distribution** and play no role in the predictive density" ([DOI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925231204005181)). **[extrapolation]** In a fixed-K neural head, a weight clamp is a crude prior; the entropy cost is the smoother version.
- **Mixture-coefficient layers / score heads (architecture-level fixes)**: LayoutMCL keeps a "mixture coefficient layer" whose softmax *down-weights unpaired predictors* so dead components don't surface at decode time — a weight-shrinkage-aware design rather than a fix of the shrinkage itself (Nguyen et al. 2023).
- **β-NLL reweighting**: Seitzer et al. (2022) — weight each point's NLL contribution by its variance^β to stop NLL from starving hard regions ([arXiv:2203.09168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09168)). Designed for heteroscedastic single-Gaussian regression; **[extrapolation]** the mixture analogue is weighting per-component gradient contributions by σₖ^β.
- **Sampling-and-fitting / EWTA (structural escape from NLL)**: Makansi et al. (2019) replace joint NLL training with an evolving-WTA sampling stage plus a separate fitting stage; their core message is that direct MDN NLL training is the problem ([arXiv:1906.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03631)).

## 5. How canonical implementations actually handle this

- **Bishop (1994)** — original MDN: σ = exp(z) ("avoids pathological configurations"), softmax for mixing coefficients, NLL error; nothing else (no floors, no clamps) ([NCRG/94-004](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/bishop-ncrg-94-004.pdf)).
- **Graves (2013)** — handwriting RNN-MDN: π via softmax (Eq. 19), σ = exp(σ̂) (Eq. 21), ρ = tanh (Eq. 22); stability via **gradient clipping** on the loss path ([−100,100] output / [−10,10] recurrent), rmsprop; still observed late-training numerical problems; sampling-time temperature-like "bias" b (Eqs. 61–62) ([arXiv:1308.0850](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0850)).
- **sketch-rnn (Ha & Eck 2017) + magenta code** — 20 bivariate components; softmax π, exp σ, tanh ρ; loss guards log(0) with `epsilon = 1e-6`; default `grad_clip=1.0`; sampling temperature τ on both π̂ and σ² ([paper arXiv:1704.03477](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03477); [model.py#L278](https://github.com/magenta/magenta/blob/main/magenta/models/sketch_rnn/model.py#L278)). No explicit σ clamp in the original code.
- **World Models MDN-RNN (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018)** — reuses the sketch-rnn MDN loss: fully log-space (`logmix + tf_lognormal` → `reduce_logsumexp`), raw `logstd` head with **no clamping** ([code carracing/rnn/rnn.py#L139](https://github.com/hardmaru/WorldModelsExperiments/blob/master/carracing/rnn/rnn.py#L139)); paper: *World Models*, NeurIPS 2018 ([arXiv:1803.10122](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122)).
- **Modern PyTorch implementations**:
  - labml annotated sketch-rnn: **explicit σ floor clamp** `torch.clamp_min(sigma, 1e-5)` + ρ clamp to avoid NaNs ([__init__.py#L165-L167](https://github.com/labmlai/annotated_deep_learning_paper_implementations/blob/90e21b5a/labml_nn/sketch_rnn/__init__.py#L165-L167)).
  - sksq96/pytorch-mdn (popular reference MDN): plain exp parameterization in notebooks, no clamps ([mdn.ipynb](https://github.com/sksq96/pytorch-mdn/blob/master/mdn.ipynb)) — representative of "works on toy problems, fragile in production".
  - Pyro `MixtureOfDiagNormals`: log-space, max-subtracted `logsumexp` — the reference stable PyTorch mixture ([diag_normal_mixture.py](https://github.com/pyro-ppl/pyro/blob/dev/pyro/distributions/diag_normal_mixture.py#L95-L106)); TFP `MixtureSameFamily` is the TF equivalent ([mixture_same_family.py](https://github.com/tensorflow/probability/blob/main/tensorflow_probability/python/distributions/mixture_same_family.py#L678)).
- **Papers that specifically analyze MDN degeneracy**: Makansi et al. 2019 (collapse + instability, EWTA fix); Zhou et al. 2020 (mode vs model collapse, entropy fix); Hjorth & Nabney 1999 (overfitting + kernel-init guidelines); Kreutz et al. 2024 (overestimated-K collapse, CSI); Letzelter et al. 2024 (survey-style catalog of MDN failure modes incl. instability, collapse, variance-gradient starvation, kernel bias); Brando Guillaumes, *Mixture Density Networks for distribution and uncertainty estimation*, MSc thesis UPC (2017) (early catalog of MDN mode collapse, cited in [arXiv:2406.04706](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04706v1)); Biernacki & Chrétien 2003 (EM degeneracy mechanics, applies to the GMM the MDN parameterizes).

## 6. Transfer analysis: 17-dim hypercube vertices + isotropic noise, 32 spherical components

Setup: targets y = v + ε, v ∈ {0,1}¹⁷, ε ~ N(0, σ_n²·I); min inter-vertex (Euclidean) distance d_min = 1; teacher's top-40 support per step ⇒ ≤ 40 active vertices per position, 32 components < 40. Let r(d,σ) = exp(−d²/(2σ²)) be the density ratio a component with std σ at one vertex puts on another vertex at distance d.

**a) σ cap relative to inter-vertex distance — the single most important control**
- r(1, 0.25) ≈ 3e-4; r(1, 0.30) ≈ 4e-3; r(1, 0.40) ≈ 4e-2; r(1, 0.50) ≈ 0.14; r(1, 0.56) ≈ 0.20. So **σ_max ≤ ~0.3 keeps a component's mass off its nearest neighbor vertices** (leakage < 1e-2), while σ_max = 0.5+ *inherently* smears across neighbors.
- The likelihood-optimal σ for a component that owns exactly one vertex is ≈ σ_n. So: if σ_n ≲ 0.2, set σ_max ≈ 0.30 (a cap that never binds at optimum, only guards transient large-σ early in training and prevents dead-zone components from inflating σ, which starves their gradients — cf. §3). **[extrapolation, grounded in the density-ratio formula and the Makansi upper-bound practice]**
- If σ_n is large (the design allows large noise variance): σ_max must be ≥ ~σ_n or you force σ below the data spread, which inflates NLL and pushes the optimizer to grow weights of other components (collapse pressure). At σ_n = 0.5, neighbor leakage ≈ 0.14 is unavoidable — smearing across *adjacent* vertices becomes a feature you tolerate (Hamming-distance-1 decode + semantic tree are the safety net per the project design), and you should then *prefer* σ_max = σ_n exactly, never larger. **[extrapolation]**
- Implement the cap in log space: clamp z = log σ to [log σ_min, log σ_max] after the linear head (σ_min ≈ 1e-3–1e-4, chosen ≪ σ_n so the floor essentially never binds; its job is bounding the NLL against the σ→0 degeneracy and against fp underflow — Biernacki & Chrétien 2003; Makansi 2019; Bishop 1994).
- **Upper cap binds ⇒ under-splitting alarm**: if a component's log σ sits at log σ_max for many steps while its weight is large, it is smearing multiple vertices — monitor this; it is the explicit "one component covers several vertices" detector (cf. (c)).

**b) Initializing component means at/near vertices — direct transfer, high value**
- With 32 components and ≤ 40 active vertices (which are themselves clustered in code space because top-40 tokens are semantically similar ⇒ Hamming-adjacent), random init is precisely the overestimated-K regime where collapse is near-certain (Kreutz et al. 2024, CSI). Transferable recipe:
  1. Collect the active-vertex set from the teacher's top-40 samples (or cluster the training targets), run k-means (or farthest-point sampling for the vertex lattice) to get 32 centers; initialize μₖ exactly at the nearest vertex (Bishop PRML §9.2.2; sklearn k-means init; Hjorth & Nabney 1999 kernel-placement guidelines).
  2. Initialize log σ = log σ_n, and π logits at uniform (or softmax over observed vertex frequencies). This makes every component start with positive responsibility — the gradient-starvation loop (Pezeshki 2021; Seitzer 2022) never gets a chance to start.
  3. Optional Makansi-style schedule: train means first (a few hundred steps of EWTA or plain NLL with σ frozen), then unfreeze σ — their exact recipe ([arXiv:1906.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03631)).
- Hjorth & Nabney's spacing formula is the theoretical anchor for σ relative to the vertex lattice: a kernel that must *bridge* two vertices d=1 apart needs σ* ≈ 0.56, which is exactly the smearing regime — i.e., bridging is the wrong optimum when per-vertex mass is high enough to justify splitting.

**c) The "one component smears across multiple vertices" under-splitting failure**
- When does merging win? A component covering a cluster of vertices with RMS radius r_c has optimal σ ≈ √(σ_n² + r_c²). Merging two vertices distance 1 apart with comparable mass forces σ ≈ 0.5–0.56 — hence the σ-cap logic above. Splitting wins when the two vertices both carry substantial teacher mass; merging is the equilibrium when one of them is rare. With 32 < 40 and semantically-adjacent codes, **some merging is the correct answer** — the failure is when a component merges *distant* vertices (Hamming ≥ 3) or when merging happens only because of starvation, not likelihood.
- Countermeasures that transfer, in order of expected value: (1) entropy cost on π (Zhou et al. 2020) — directly counteracts weight shrinkage so rare-vertex components survive; (2) DAEM-style annealing of the π-logit temperature from β ≫ 1 down to 1 (Ueda & Nakano 1998; Rose 1998; aWTA 2024 is the modern neural variant) — gives rare vertices a chance to acquire components early; (3) greedy growth: start with fewer components and split high-weight/at-cap components (Vlassis & Likas 2002) — or simply raise K; (4) k-means init per (b) so the initial Voronoi partition matches the vertex distribution.
- Note the regime-specific escape hatch: because codes are assigned by the semantic tree (min Hamming 1), a smeared component's *mean* still lies at/near a vertex, and decode (weight-pick → discretize mean) lands on a semantically plausible token even when the component covers neighbors. The hard failure is weight shrinkage (a vertex's mass evaporating entirely), not smearing per se. **[extrapolation from the design in AGENTS.md]**

**d) Everything else, briefly**
- **fp32 loss, always**: compute per-component log-density and logsumexp in fp32 outside autocast (Pyro/TFP pattern; Micikevicius 2018; PyTorch amp op policy). With σ_n ≥ 1e-2 and σ_min ≥ 1e-3, fp32 is sufficient; fp64 only needed if you drop σ_min further. **[extrapolation: fp64 necessity]**
- **Temperature scaling of component logits**: at decode time, τ on π (sketch-rnn Eq. 8 / Graves bias) modulates token diversity — cheap and useful for the milestone "semantically correct sentences" (τ ≲ 1 favors high-probability tokens). At train time, the same knob is the annealing schedule of (c).
- **Minimum-weight clamps**: skip the hard clamp; the entropy cost (Zhou 2020) or a Dirichlet-style prior (Svensén & Bishop 2005, where surplus components revert to the prior) is the principled version, and in a fixed-32-component head the entropy cost with a small weight (start w ≈ 0.01–0.1 × NLL scale; Zhou used w_model = 50 at a different loss scale — **tune, and treat these numbers as untransferable**) is the pragmatic choice. **[extrapolation on the weight value]**
- **β-NLL**: adapt only if you see a component's σ explode while its weight stays high (the heteroscedastic starvation pattern); per-component σₖ^β weighting is a direct analogue of Seitzer et al. 2022. **[extrapolation]**
- **What does *not* transfer**: WTA/EWTA sampling-and-fitting (Makansi 2019) — you have a *given* teacher distribution to distill, so you can afford direct NLL; EWTA is only a fallback if NLL training proves unstable in practice. Hjorth & Nabney's Bayesian evidence regularization is overkill for a frozen-backbone distillation. Graves-style gradient clipping should be unnecessary given the two-sided σ clamp; keep it as a cheap safety net.

**Bottom-line design recipe (all pieces cited above)**: log-σ head clamped to [log 1e-3, log σ_max] with σ_max = max(0.30, σ_n·1.2); k-means-to-vertex μ init + σ init = σ_n + uniform π; entropy cost on π; DAEM-style π-logit temperature annealed 2→1 over the first epochs; NLL computed in fp32 log-space via logsumexp; monitor σ-at-cap and per-component responsibility mass as the under-splitting/weight-shrinkage alarm.
